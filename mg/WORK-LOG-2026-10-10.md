## 3. T77: one consistency owner for authority changes

### 3.1 What the code did (read 2026-10-10)

Sessions (`sessions.json`) carry a copy of the account's authority (`role`, `assignedAgents`,
`canAuthorPolicy`, `managedBy`) so a check costs no read. Every change wrote the account first
and patched the copy afterwards, at the route, in a separate locked write:

| Change                                               | Account write                                      | Session copy                                                              | If the copy failed                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Role (incl. demotion, move to another Administrator) | `setUserRole`                                      | `updateSessionsRoleForUser` + `updateSessionsAssignedAgents` at the route | 500; demoted account keeps its old role in live sessions for up to 12 h |
| Policy authoring                                     | `setUserPolicyAuthoring`                           | `updateSessionsPolicyAuthoring` at the route                              | 500; withheld authoring still usable                                    |
| Assignment                                           | `assignAgentsToAccount`                            | `updateSessionsAssignedAgents` at the route                               | 500; revoked agent still reachable                                      |
| Ownership transfer, unregistration                   | `revokeHoldersOutsideOwner`, one account at a time | `updateSessionsAssignedAgents` per account                                | the loop stops half way                                                 |
| Password reset                                       | `setUserPassword`                                  | `revokeSessionsForUser` at the route, after                               | 500; old cookies (perhaps the attacker's) still work                    |
| Organisation deletion                                | `deleteGroupAccounts`                              | revoked after the point of no return                                      | reported "stays signed in until the session expires"                    |

And three joins across separately locked files: `assignAgentsToAccount` validated the agents
against the registry and the route's **snapshot** of the account (its `managedBy`) and then
wrote under the users lock only; `setAgentOwner` read every account **before** taking any lock
and revoked holders from that snapshot (its doc comment: "an ownership change racing an
assignment can land after the check … which `setAgentOwner` then repairs on its own next pass",
which exists only if the agent changes owner again); `assertOwnershipSurvives` (381, 382) ran at
the route, outside every lock ("a concurrent re-own in the gap is the accepted tradeoff"). Sign-in
(`authenticate` then `issueSession`) rechecked only that the account still exists (T76, D14).

### 3.2 Design (decided by precedent: T76's revocation inside the commit, D11 and D14)

- **One owner per change: the store function that writes the account** also writes the session
  copy, inside the users-file lock, in an order that never leaves a session broader than the
  account: (a) every session of the account set to the **narrower of the old and the new
  authority** (lower role, intersection of agents, authoring only if both allow); (b) the account
  written; (c) the sessions set to the new authority. (a) failing: nothing changes, a typed error
  (503 at the route, T76's `sessions_unavailable`). (b) failing: the account is unchanged and its
  sessions narrower than it, the restrictive direction, and the error is reported. (c) failing:
  the change stands, the sessions hold the narrower authority until their holder signs in again,
  and the response and the ledger entry say so (`sessionsError`), never a 500 for a change that
  happened (T76's point-of-no-return rule). No rollback, no second file.
- **One lock order across the three files: agent registry, then accounts, then sessions.**
  Every change that checks one file to write another holds the locks it reads: assignment
  (registry checked against the account **as it is inside the lock**), ownership transfer and
  unregistration (accounts read and holders released inside the registry lock, in one accounts
  write), registration (owner eligibility inside the registry lock), role change and deletion
  (`assertOwnershipSurvives` inside both locks). Nothing takes them the other way round.
- **Sign-in confirms its session under the accounts lock**: after issuing, the account is re-read
  inside the lock; gone, or its password changed since it was verified: the session is revoked and
  the sign-in refused; otherwise the session is given the account's authority as it is now.
- **Password reset and organisation deletion** take T76's order: sessions revoked inside the
  accounts lock before the write (failure: nothing changed), swept again after.
